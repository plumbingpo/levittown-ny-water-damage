Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. We have ordered these five common Brooklyn calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. In Brooklyn kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Avoid wiping or bleaching a big area on your own, as that sends spores into other rooms.
