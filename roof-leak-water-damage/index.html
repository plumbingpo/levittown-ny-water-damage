Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Levittown

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Cavities

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

Trace the Leak Path

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How far down the leak traveled, from the attic to the walls and floor.
  • Finish work, including matching an existing texture and painting the room.
  • The length of time the leak went unnoticed, plus whether mold has started.
  • Drywall removal: how much of the ceiling and walls is beyond saving.
